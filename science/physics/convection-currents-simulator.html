The Physics of Convection

1. Thermal Expansion: When the fluid is heated, particles gain kinetic energy, push apart, and volume (V) increases.

2. Buoyant Force: Following Archimedes' principle, the lower density (ρ) heated fluid experiences an upward buoyant force from the cooler, denser fluid sinking around it.

3. Mass Conservation: As hot fluid rises, cooler fluid must flow in from the sides to replace it. This creates a continuous circulatory loop known as a Convection Cell.

Note: This simulator uses the Boussinesq approximation, where density differences are ignored except where they multiply gravity to create buoyancy forces.

Teacher's Challenge:

Clear the fluid, then set the heat source to "Top". Notice how the hot fluid stays at the top? This is called thermal stratification and is why heating a room from the ceiling is inefficient!
