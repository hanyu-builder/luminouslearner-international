The Specificity of Enzymes

Biology Virtual Labs - Part 1: Lock & Key Hypothesis

Why are enzymes specific?

Enzymes are highly specific biological catalysts. They do not react with just any molecule; they only react with their designated substrate.

The Active Site

The enzyme (purple) has a uniquely shaped pocket called the Active Site. Its 3D geometry is perfectly complementary to its specific substrate.

The "Key"

Only the complementary substrate (cyan curve) can fit into the active site to form an Enzyme-Substrate Complex.

Incompatible Shapes

Other molecules, like the red cube or orange pyramid, have the wrong shape. They may collide with the enzyme but cannot bind to the active site.
