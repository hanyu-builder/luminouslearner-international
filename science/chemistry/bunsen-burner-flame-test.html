Lab Reference Manual

1. Proper Cleaning Procedure

Platinum or nichrome wires easily accumulate sodium impurities from fingers and dust. You must dip the wire in concentrated Hydrochloric Acid (HCl) and hold it in the roaring blue flame until it burns completely clear. Repeating this is required between every test to prevent cross-contamination.

2. Bunsen Burner Physics

The air hole controls oxygen flow. Closed hole: Incomplete combustion. Produces a yellow, "luminous" and sooty flame (~300°C) which is useless for flame tests because the yellow soot masks the ion colors. Open hole: Complete combustion. Produces a blue, "roaring" non-luminous flame (~1500°C) which is hot enough to excite metal electrons.

3. The Cobalt Glass Filter

Because sodium is a very common impurity and burns with a blindingly bright yellow flame, it often completely masks the pale lilac flame of Potassium. Looking through a piece of blue Cobalt Glass absorbs the yellow wavelengths of light, allowing the red/blue wavelengths of Potassium to pass through to your eye.
